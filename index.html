01 / FORECASTING→

Australia's renewable transition

A dashboard tracking Australia's shift to renewable electricity from 2005 to 2024, and where it lands by 2035.

  • Traced renewable share from 9% in 2005 to 35% in 2024 — solar alone grew from 0.1 to 48.6 TWh
  • Forecast 44.6% renewable share by 2035 using R linear regression (R² ≈ 0.85, p < 0.001)
  • Found Australia falls well short of its 82%-by-2030 target on current trajectory
  • Benchmarked against Germany (54.5%) and New Zealand (85.5%) to put the gap in context

Power BI · DAX · R · Time-series forecasting

05

How I work

  1. Clean it before you model it

    On FreshMart I audited five source tables before touching the schema — duplicate customers, three spellings of the same category, 93 rows pointing at a customer who didn't exist. A dashboard built on unaudited data is confidently wrong, which is worse than no dashboard.

  2. Say what the analysis can't tell you

    My renewable forecast is a linear regression, so I wrote in the report that it should be read as a planning signal, not a prediction, and that including the slow early years makes it deliberately conservative. Stating the limits is how the rest of the number earns trust.

  3. Choose the method that shows its working

    I forecast in R rather than Power BI's built-in tool specifically because R prints the equation, R² and p-value onto the chart. If someone senior wants to check my reasoning, they shouldn't have to ask me for it.

  4. Decide who the answer is for

    A star schema is for the next analyst; an executive dashboard is for someone with four minutes between meetings. Same data, different job. Most analysis fails at this step, not at the maths.
