05

How I think about data

  1. Clean it before you model it

    On FreshMart I audited five source tables before touching the schema — duplicate customers, three spellings of the same category, 93 rows pointing at a customer who didn't exist. A dashboard built on unaudited data is confidently wrong, which is worse than no dashboard.

  2. Say what the analysis can't tell you

    My renewable forecast is a linear regression, so I wrote in the report that it should be read as a planning signal, not a prediction, and that including the slow early years makes it deliberately conservative. Stating the limits is how the rest of the number earns trust.

  3. Choose the method that shows its working

    I forecast in R rather than Power BI's built-in tool specifically because R prints the equation, R² and p-value onto the chart. If someone senior wants to check my reasoning, they shouldn't have to ask me for it.

  4. Decide who the answer is for

    A star schema is for the next analyst; an executive dashboard is for someone with four minutes between meetings. Same data, different job.

06

Questions

Are these solo or group projects?

FreshMart and the renewable dashboard are mine end to end — ETL, modelling, DAX, forecasting, report. The football club database was a three-person group project where I worked across the design, schema and SQL. WeatherWise started from a provided starter template; the visualisations, caching, forecast indexing and interface are my own. Each case study says so on the page.

Can I see the working, not just the result?

Yes. Every project links to its repository, and they include the full reports, the DAX measures, the R forecasting script, the Oracle DDL, and a data decisions log recording every cleaning choice and why it was made.

Why data, after a statistics and finance degree?

The statistics is the reason. A regression is easy to run and easy to misread — the degree is what tells me when a model is telling me something and when it's telling me nothing. The masters is where I picked up the tools to put that in front of people.
